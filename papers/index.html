LEO / NTN: orbit, topology, and validation

SOURCE NOTE
How long can you trust a Starlink TLE?

Dimitrije Jankovic · 2026 · arXiv preprint v1

Why it matters: the paper reports a cohort-specific comparison of SGP4 and high-fidelity propagation from public TLE inputs, motivating Nimbus’s question about how TLE staleness, altitude shell, and platform generation should be recorded before reuse.

Does not establish: independent orbit truth, conjunction safety, operator-grade orbit determination, or a Nimbus reproduction. The paper uses the next TLE as proxy truth and is scoped to its Starlink cohort and observation window.

LEO / NTNnot independently reproduced
SOURCE NOTE
xeoverse: A Real-time Simulation Platform for Large LEO Satellite Mega-Constellations

Mohamed M. Kassem and Nishanth Sastry · 2024 · IFIP/IEEE Networking 2024

Why it matters: a simulator-design input for replayable topology deltas, changing ISL links, user terminals, satellites, and ground stations. It informs what a future LEO network fixture would need to make its topology and route assumptions inspectable.

Does not establish: Nimbus has executed the artifact, reproduced its speed or fidelity figures, or validated weather, routing, or protocol behaviour in a real constellation.

LEO / NTNpaper-reported performance
SOURCE NOTE
An SDR-Based Test Platform for 5G NTN Prototyping and Validation

Lu Hou, Kan Zheng, Jie Mei, and Cheng Huang · 2025 · IEEE Open Journal of the Communications Society

Why it matters: it supplies a protocol-to-testbed-to-field-validation framing: application KPI claims should retain propagation, pacing, buffering, traffic, and stack context rather than being treated as generic “NTN performance.”

Does not establish: independent validation of the reported throughput, round-trip time, BLER, or SNR; it is not evidence that Nimbus operated the platform or that results generalise to all NTN deployments.

Agent evaluation: gains, regressions, and verification

SOURCE NOTE
The Regression Tax: Decomposing Why Skills Help and Hurt LLM Agents

Darshan Tank and Baran Nama · 2026 · arXiv preprint v1

Why it matters: it motivates Nimbus’s practice of tracking regressions alongside apparent gains: a procedure can improve one task while displacing grounding or verification on another. This informs the route-aware fixture’s emphasis on evidence and post-state checks.

Does not establish: that its nearly 6,000-run findings apply to Nimbus, every model, or every harness. Nimbus has not reproduced the benchmark, its controls, or its reported effects.

Agent × network operations: bounded actuation

SOURCE NOTE
Agentic Open RAN: A Deterministic and Auditable Framework for Intent-Driven Radio Control

Hengxu Li, Dongkuan Xu, Mingzhe Chen, and Yuchen Liu · 2026 · accepted at IEEE ICC 2026

Why it matters: the paper’s described intent → typed policy → bounded deterministic executor → logged outcome split is a useful design reference for keeping language-model reasoning separate from safety-sensitive execution.

Does not establish: safe autonomous radio control, deployment readiness, independently reproduced KPI results, or a production control recommendation by Nimbus.
