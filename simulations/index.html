Case study 001 · synthetic fixture

Route-aware fixture,
bounded conclusion.

This synthetic fixture tests whether an evaluator can distinguish unsafe actions, safe alternates, fallback, probe, and refusal. It is a test artifact—not a real network controller or a deployment simulation.

Why simulate this

In a safety evaluation, an answer can look successful while taking the wrong route: a safe alternate may be ignored, a fallback may be mislabeled, or a refusal may mask missing evidence. A small synthetic fixture makes those route distinctions inspectable before anyone treats an aggregate score as meaningful.

Method → result → limit

INPUTS
Constructed action routes and a versioned manifest.

The fixture uses deliberately constructed tasks and labels. It is not a trace of a customer network, a satellite system, or a production agent.

METHOD
Route-contract audit plus deterministic validation.

The local check runs route-contract coverage, four conversion tests, syntax compilation, and revision validation. It checks that the fixture retained intended route distinctions after conversion.

OBSERVED
The bounded fixture passed its integrity checks.

All 100 route contracts and the stated local checks passed for the current fixture revision.

local check
LIMIT
No model ranking or real-world performance inference.

The result does not establish model-family rates, generalisation, a workshop-paper claim, controller reliability, or field deployment safety. Those would require a different design and evidence.
